# ERP correlates of metaphor vs literal language: component and timing survey

**Scope.** Text-only review of article/abstract/method pages accessed 2026-09-30. Included 12 EEG/ERP reports (2002-2024), prioritizing 2005-2024; the one 2002 report is retained as a foundational comparison. Tasks are overwhelmingly comprehension. A separate production-relevant study by Zhao et al. (2011) is noted below; it compares two metaphor-reasoning types, not metaphor with literal production, so it is outside the 12-study metaphor-vs-literal comparison table. I found no eligible scalp-ERP study that directly contrasts spontaneous metaphor production with literal production. “n/a” means the article page/abstract/method text inspected here did not state the detail. “Window basis n/a” means I could not verify whether it was set a priori or after inspection. **Production-relevant ERP record outside the 12 direct comparisons:** [Zhao et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22018690/), *Brain Research*, DOI [10.1016/j.brainres.2011.09.041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2011.09.041). Chinese verbal analogical reasoning used word pairs; the answer-production stage is the lock for P2/LPC effects (exact event onset n/a). Reference, channel count, windows and sites n/a on the PubMed abstract. Components in abstract: N400 (negative, greater over right anterior scalp during schema induction; broadly distributed at analogy mapping), P300 (positive, widely distributed), LNC1/LNC2 (negative; left anterior for LNC1), P2 (positive; answer production), and LPC (positive; larger for relational than attributive metaphor). It has no literal condition, so it does not inform metaphor-vs-literal contrasts.

## Cross-study summary

- **N400 - 12/12 comparison reports analyzed it:** reported windows span 300-560 ms where specified; most often approximately 300-500 ms. Sites range from centro-parietal/right-biased or widespread to frontal/central/parietal groupings; no single robust lateralization. A metaphor-literal difference was reported in 10/12 studies; direction often more negative for metaphor, but not universal (e.g. early/localized only in De Grauwe; no simple direct contrast in Weiland/Obert abstracts; Chinese verb effects depend on metaphor subtype). Studies: Coulson & Van Petten 2002; Lai 2009; De Grauwe 2010; Nam & Jeong 2016; Rutter 2012; Weiland 2014; Tang et al. 2017; Obert et al. 2018; Bambini et al. 2019; Li et al. 2022; Tang et al. 2022; Baiocco et al. 2024.
- **P600/LPC or late positivity — at least 7/12**: explicit windows span 450–900 ms where reported; commonly the later 550–900 ms interval. Reported distributions include posterior/parietal, frontal, and broad scalp; Baiocco’s late polarity changed with task. Metaphor-literal differences occurred in Coulson & Van Petten 2002, De Grauwe 2010, Nam 2016, Tang 2017, Li 2022, and Baiocco 2024; Obert 2018’s abstract reports late frontal positivity for novel metaphor but does not make the literal contrast explicit.
- **Late/sustained negativity — at least 5/12**: 450–900 ms where specified; frontal in Bambini and left-anterior for Baiocco’s judgment task; Tang 2017 reported a bilateral late negativity in the LPC interval. Differences from literal depended on novelty/context and task; some studies report the late effect as reduced LPC rather than naming a separate negativity. Studies: Rutter 2012, Tang 2017, Bambini 2019, Tang 2022, Baiocco 2024.
- **Production / verb-lock relevance:** the closest direct precedent is Li et al. 2022, which separately locks Chinese metaphor comprehension ERPs to the verb and object. It analyzes verb-locked N400 (380–500 ms) and P600/LPC (670–770 ms) at frontal/central/parietal sites, with verb-type-specific patterns. This supports analyzing the verb onset independently from later object integration, while its comprehension task and subject–verb/verb–object materials differ from production. Zhao et al. 2011 includes answer production in verbal analogical reasoning and reports P2 at answer production, but its metaphor conditions are not contrasted against literal language; it is not a direct production-vs-literal template. Among the reviewed direct metaphor/literal comparisons, production remains an evidence gap.
